Every term the simulation puts on screen, and ten things about the real industry that are worth knowing. Print it or save it as a PDF to keep.

1Weight and measurement
Gross weightactual
What the consignment really weighs on the scale, in kilos.
Volumetric weightdimensional
What the consignment weighs for charging purposes given the space it occupies: length × width × height in centimetres, divided by a million, times the factor. A box of feathers pays for the room it takes, not for its scale weight.
Volumetric factor167 kg/m³
The conversion IATA sets between volume and chargeable kilos: 167 kilos per cubic metre, the same thing as the older 6,000 cm³ per kilo.
Chargeable weight
The greater of gross and volumetric, rounded up to the next half kilo. Almost every charge on the quotation is calculated on this figure, not on the real weight.
Rate band
Air freight is priced in steps by weight: under 100 kg, 100–299, 300–499, and 500 and over. The price per kilo falls as the shipment gets heavier.
Minimum charge
The floor below which a shipment cannot be quoted, however light it is. A 3 kg parcel to Lisbon rates 28.35 by the kilo but is billed at the 45.00 minimum.
Weight breakkey concept
When declaring a heavier weight costs less, because it falls into a cheaper band. 95 kg to Lisbon rates 897.75 in its own band, but rated as 100 kg it costs 300.00. The engine spots this and quotes the lower figure. Standard industry practice, not a trick.
Rate classM · N · Q
The letter on the air waybill saying how the shipment was priced. M when the minimum charge applies, N for a normal rate under 100 kg, Q for a quantity rate at 100 kg or more.
2Documents and codes
Air waybillAWB
The contract of carriage between shipper and airline, and the receipt for the goods. Unlike a sea bill of lading it is not negotiable: it does not represent title to the goods and cannot be traded or endorsed to a third party.
Master air waybillMAWB
The waybill the airline issues to the forwarder for the whole consignment. Every quotation in this simulation is a single MAWB.
Airline prefix083
The three digits that open every air waybill number and identify the carrier. The number then runs 083 - 1234 5678, and that last digit is a check digit: the remainder of dividing the seven preceding digits by seven.
HS codecustoms
Harmonised System code, the international classification of goods. Six digits agreed worldwide, extended nationally. Customs rates the duty on this, so it belongs on the consignment line.
Charge codes
The abbreviations on the breakdown, so the quotation reads like a real one: WT air freight, ETS emissions, XR screening, AP airport, CC export customs, AW waybill fee, TH handling at origin, DGO and DGD dangerous goods, SD security on arrival, CH import clearance, DB import documents, LB truck loading, TD handling at destination, ST storage.
3The cargo itself
Loose cargobulk
Cargo loaded piece by piece rather than in a container. SDG Airlines carries loose cargo only.
ULDnot offered here
Unit Load Device: the containers and pallets built to the curve of an aircraft hold. Faster to load, but they have to be positioned, tracked and returned.
Dangerous goodsDGR
Anything classified as hazardous in transport — batteries, aerosols, chemicals. Needs a shipper's declaration, trained staff and segregation in the warehouse, so it carries a higher handling tariff and an acceptance fee at each end.
Cool chamber
Temperature-controlled storage for pharmaceuticals and perishables. The most expensive storage tariff and the fewest free days, because the room is scarce and costly to run.
Free days
The grace period before storage starts being charged: five days for general cargo, four for cool chamber, six for dangerous goods.
4Trade terms and the network
Incoterms 2020
The ICC rules that say where the seller's costs and risk end and the buyer's begin. They do not change what a shipment costs — only who pays which part of it.
FCAFree Carrier
The seller delivers the goods cleared for export to the carrier. The buyer contracts and pays the air carriage and everything after it.
CPT and CIP
Carriage Paid To, and the same with insurance added. The seller pays the flight to the destination airport; the buyer takes the charges waiting there. Risk still passes at origin, which is what surprises people.
DAPDelivered At Place
The seller carries the cost all the way to the buyer's premises, but the buyer clears the import and pays the duty.
DDPDelivered Duty Paid
The seller bears everything, duties and taxes included. The heaviest obligation of the eleven Incoterms.
Schengen and WCO
Inside the Schengen area there is no customs frontier, so clearance, the customs cost and the import paperwork simply do not arise. Elsewhere the WCO framework applies and they do.
Rotation, leg and hub
A leg is one flight between two stations. A rotation is the closed loop an aircraft flies before starting again. A hub is the station a service is built around.
SDRSpecial Drawing Right
The IMF's unit of account, used to express carrier liability so the figure does not depend on any one currency.
5Ten things about the real industry

  1. Air cargo moves less than 1% of world trade by volume but more than a third of it by value — around 8.3 trillion dollars of goods a year, some 22.7 billion every day, on roughly 62 million tonnes.

    IATA, Value of Air Cargo

  2. If an airline loses or damages your cargo, its liability is capped by weight, not by what the goods were worth. The ceiling rose to 26 SDR per kilogram on 28 December 2024, about 35 US dollars, and is reviewed every five years. Declare a higher value or insure the shipment, or a pallet of microchips is worth the same as a pallet of bricks.

    ICAO / Montreal Convention 1999, liability limit revision

  3. The air waybill is not negotiable. A sea bill of lading can be endorsed and traded while the ship is at sea — the document is the goods. An air waybill is only a contract and a receipt, which is why air shipments are usually paid for differently.

    Montreal Convention 1999, Articles 4–11

  4. The last digit of every air waybill number is a check digit: divide the seven-digit serial by seven and the remainder is that digit. It exists so a number mistyped down a phone line fails immediately instead of misrouting a shipment.

    IATA Cargo Services Conference, resolution on AWB numbering

  5. The volumetric divisor is a convention, not physics. Air freight uses 6,000 cm³ per kilo, or 167 kg/m³; road and express operators commonly use 5,000 or 4,000, which is why the same box can have three different chargeable weights depending on how it travels.

    IATA TACT Rules

  6. A large share of the world's air cargo never flies on a freighter: it rides in the belly holds of passenger aircraft. When passenger flying stopped in 2020 that capacity vanished overnight and rates went up sharply, even though there was no shortage of aircraft.

    IATA, Cargo programme

  7. The dangerous goods rules are reissued every year. An edition of the IATA DGR manual is valid for one calendar year, and shipping to last year's edition is a rejection at acceptance, not a warning.

    IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, annual edition

  8. Rating a shipment as heavier than it is to pay less is legitimate and expected. The weight break is written into the tariff structure; a forwarder who does not apply it is simply overcharging the customer.

    IATA TACT Rules, quantity rates

  9. Four Incoterms — FAS, FOB, CFR and CIF — are for sea and inland waterway only. They are used on air shipments constantly anyway, and every time it happens the contract says something the parties did not mean, because they refer to goods crossing a ship's rail.

    ICC, Incoterms® 2020

  10. Airport codes come in two flavours. IATA codes are three letters and commercial — BCN, LIS. ICAO codes are four letters and operational, and their first letters encode the region: LE for mainland Spain, LP for Portugal, so Barcelona is LEBL and Lisbon LPPT.

    ICAO Doc 7910, Location Indicators

Figures are those published at the time of writing and are reviewed periodically by the bodies cited — check the source before quoting them. The tariffs inside the simulation are invented; everything on this page is not.
